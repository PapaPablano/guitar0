Grounding Context

Tab Highway is a guitar-tab practice app (tab highway, fretboard, recording playback with an offset slider, WebCodecs video export). The plan adds a Windows desktop build that carries a pinned, unmodified StemDeck engine (htdemucs_6s: vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano, other), so a player can separate their own recording locally, mute or solo the guitar against the tab, and export the mix.

  • Patterns: pure functions for controls, gains and rendering; one clock per audio source; a bridge that tolerates the absence of Tauri so the web build is unchanged; a content-hash index for saved stems.
  • Known pains: one shared offset capped at 10 s that drifts when a recording speeds up or slows down; six media elements resynced every 20 ms (40 ms tolerance) with slow-tempo drift flagged as the plan's own risk; first-run model download with no resume and a fixed 90 s engine health wait; a 360 s export cap.
  • Outside signal: stem mute, slow-down and loop are table stakes (Moises, StemDeck's own mixer, small apps). The gaps found were a tab highway combined with the user's own stems, stem mix in tab video export, and a guitar-amount mode tied to the tab. Guitar Pro 8 aligns a recording with user-placed Sync Points. The 6-stem model's guitar stem is documented as "okay" and guitar-removed backing leaves residue.
  • Boundaries held: Windows only; no stem features on the web site; unmodified pinned engine; no YouTube, song library or BPM auto-alignment.

Ranked Ideas

Top pick: anchor-point alignment. It fixes the one limit the app already admits to in its own UI. Ghost guitar is the cheapest high-value move.

1. Anchor-point alignment on the tab clock

Axis: Alignment of recording to tabConfidence 75%Complexity Medium-High
Description
Replace the single offset with 2-4 user-placed anchors ("this beat is bar 17"). Build the timeline's tempo map from the anchors so the highway and fretboard follow the performer, and leave the stems unstretched so the existing 40 ms resync is untouched. The slider stays as the one-anchor case. Allow a pre-roll or tab-side shift so a recording that starts late can be aligned. It is manual alignment, not BPM auto-alignment.
Basis
direct: src/app/OffsetSlider.tsx:54 says "One offset aligns the whole recording, so a recording that speeds up or slows down against the tab will drift." RANGE_SECONDS = 10 at line 13, and user-audio.ts:97 clamps with Math.max(0, seconds). alphatab-adapter.ts:9 already converts ticks to seconds through a piecewise tempo map, so an anchor list has the same shape on the media side. external: Guitar Pro 8 Audio Track Sync Points; subtitle tools that interpolate between anchor points.
Rationale
Live takes drift, and no slider setting fixes a recording that rushes a chorus. The verifier found the tab-clock variant sound and the audio-side variant costly: the offset appears in the user-audio, stem-mix, stem-audio and export paths, so warping audio means touching all of them.
Downsides
A timeline rebuilt from anchors must fit the immutable Timeline and alphaTab playback, which is unverified. Loop bars map through the anchors, so loop wrap needs care. Drift visibility (an onset or waveform lane under the tab, computed in the page) is a natural follow-up, not part of this idea.

2. Ghost guitar: a guitar-amount control instead of mute

Axis: Practice mixer and loop mechanicsConfidence 80%Complexity Low
Description
One control from full guitar, through a faint ghost, to gone. Variants to choose between: scale the guitar stem to 10-25%; crossfade the guitar stem against the tab's own synth guitar so the player hears the exact notes; or build the backing as the original minus a share of the guitar stem so separation residue stays in the mix. Optionally blend the "other" stem back. The change lives in the pure gain function, and export reuses it.
Basis
direct: src/audio/mix-gains.ts:26-34 computes gains[name] = s.muted || (anySolo && !s.solo) ? 0 : clampVolume(s.volume), and src/export/stem-audio.ts:25 shares it. external: the web research found htdemucs_6s guitar "okay" with residue after removal, and no incumbent offers a guitar-amount mode tied to a tab; Music Minus One records are the long-standing analogy.
Rationale
A hard mute exposes the model's weakest output. A ghost level hides the artifacts and keeps a guide under the player. It is a small change to a pure function that reaches playback, export and tests at once.
Downsides
The synth crossfade needs the synth clock to run beside the stem clock, which has no precedent in the code. Residue quality was not measured, so the "original minus guitar" variant is a hypothesis to try by ear.

3. Practice profile saved per recording

Axis: Saved stems and song identityConfidence 70%Complexity Medium
Description
Widen the saved-stems index from hash -> jobId to a small record per file: job, offset or anchors (idea 1), named mix scenes such as "Play along" and "Compare", last loop, tempo. Reopening the same file restores the alignment and the mix. The file's content hash is the key, so there is no library to browse. Later extensions: flag shaky bars and resurface them, or share the settings as a file with no audio.
Basis
direct: src/stems/saved-stems.ts:14-52 types the index as Record<string,string>; :36-44 guards "so a finished separation is never lost to an index error"; src/app/App.tsx:151-168 shows stems inherit live settings on activation and nothing is persisted. external: DAW scenes and per-song presets.
Rationale
Alignment is the costliest thing a user does by hand, and it is thrown away every session. The content-addressed key and atomic index write already exist.
Downsides
Needs a versioned schema and migration of existing stem-index.json. The record now holds more than a pointer, so a failed write costs more and the "never lose results" rule must extend to it. The spaced-resurfacing and share-file extensions stretch toward the deferred library and should wait.

4. Scaffold fade: the mix changes on each loop pass

Axis: Practice mixer and loop mechanicsConfidence 60%Complexity Medium
Description
Let the loop automate the mix. Options: the guitar steps down on each pass (100, 60, 25, 0%) so the recording carries the player and then leaves them alone; a held key brings it back to check an answer; a listen-then-play mode alternates a solo-guitar pass with a guitar-muted pass; optionally the speed rises a few percent per clean pass as the guitar fades. One pure per-pass gain schedule drives all of these.
Basis
direct: src/audio/stem-mix.ts:81-83 wraps loops at the leader; gains are a pure function and change "without restarting playback"; App.tsx:202 already drives rate with clock?.setRate(tempoPercent / 100). external: the guidance hypothesis in motor learning (Salmoni, Schmidt and Winstein, 1984) and cloze fading in language learning support withdrawing help progressively. StemDeck and the other tools surveyed offer only a static mute.
Rationale
It turns the mixer into a trainer, a combination only an app that holds both the tab and the stems can offer.
Downsides
The verifier rated this weak-to-sound. The motor-learning basis is a hypothesis, not proof that players benefit. Loop-wrap events are visible only inside the leader's clock, so a callback must be added. "Clean pass" needs a manual tap, because inferring it would need input analysis.

5. Export the mix as audio, or export just the loop

Axis: Export and outputConfidence 70%Complexity Low-Medium
Description
Add "save this mix as audio" (the song without guitar, for a phone or a rig) that skips video and WebCodecs entirely, and a range export of the current loop or bar range for audio or video. A longer-term form repeats the range N times with the guitar stepping down (the drill form of idea 4).
Basis
direct: src/export/stem-audio.ts:11-13 already renders the mix offline into one buffer; src/export/presets.ts:20 refuses songs over MAX_EXPORT_SECONDS "to keep memory in check"; ExportDialog.tsx:44 sets tooLong; src/export/capability.ts:23 says "Video export needs desktop Chrome or Edge". plan.md:136 marks WebCodecs inside the desktop webview unverified, a risk that sits only on the video path.
Rationale
It delivers the headline output without the plan's biggest unverified assumption, and range export lifts the long-song refusal for the part players practice. The verifier rated audio export sound and the cheapest delivery.
Downsides
Audio-only export still obeys the 360 s memory cap unless the renderer streams in blocks (a larger change, rejected below). Range export must apply the shared offset the same way playback does.

6. A first run and engine that recover

Axis: Separation engine and first runConfidence 55%Complexity Medium
Description
Make the two moments every user passes through survive failure. Show real progress and an ETA where the engine can report it; make the engine health wait scale with the machine instead of a fixed 90 s; on a post-setup crash restart only the engine, once, automatically, with the log tail visible; keep synth and tab practice usable while setup runs. A stronger form pre-seeds the model cache from a resumable, hash-checked download, but only if the engine's cache location can be set without modifying it.
Basis
direct: src-tauri/src/setup.rs:135-161 treats the model warm-up as a black box with no resume; engine.rs:140 hard-codes a 90 s health wait; main.rs:18-25 sends an engine-error state back through engine_setup(); setup.rs:47-101 already reports FFmpeg progress and checksums. external: launcher-style resumable, chunk-hashed downloads.
Rationale
The first run is a ~700 MB install plus a model download, where a flaky connection or slow machine ends the experience before it starts.
Downsides
The verifier rated the strong form weak: owning the weight download changes what the "unmodified pinned engine" runs, and that tension needs a decision. The lighter, safer parts (timeout, auto-retry, restart-only-engine, progress) carry most of the value.

7. Measure drift first, then remove it at the source

Axis: Practice mixer and loop mechanicsConfidence 50%Complexity Medium-High
Description
Treat the plan's own risk as a measured gate. If the 50% tempo manual check fails, bake the stems once at the chosen practice tempo with the already-bundled ffmpeg (atempo), play them at rate 1.0 and cache per tempo, with the live slider keeping today's path as a preview. The heavier escape hatch is one audio context with sample-accurate sources and a pitch-preserving worklet stretcher, which costs roughly 760 MB of decoded audio for a 6-minute song.
Basis
direct: src/audio/stem-mix.ts:6-9 sets DRIFT_TOLERANCE_SECONDS = 0.04 and RESYNC_MS = 20; :73-79 sets each follower's rate and pitch preservation on every change; the plan's Risks section names the worklet stretch as the fallback "if unacceptable". external: ffmpeg atempo filter.
Rationale
Slow practice at 50-70% is the core use, and it is where the plan admits the mixer is weakest. Baking avoids tuning around the problem.
Downsides
Whether the drift is audible has not been measured, so this may never be needed. Baking costs disk and time on each tempo change. Included as a gated contingency, not a commitment.

Rejection Summary

#IdeaReason rejected
1Drift lens: onset or waveform lane under the tab, with snapping anchorsBetter as a follow-up to idea 1 than as a standalone idea. Compute it in the page from the decoded stem; the verifier found no evidence the engine environment carries analysis dependencies.
2Wrapper "analysis lane" routes (onsets, peaks, loudness)Basis weak: the cited guard.py is a secret-header and CORS gate, not a route file, and the analysis-dependency premise is unverified. Duplicates the in-page option above.
3Practice-first separation (separate the loop first, swap in the full song)Real pain, but unverified that the pinned engine accepts clips and gives clean stems at clip edges; the slice must not be indexed as the file's stems. Revisit after real separation times are measured on the target CPU.
4Engine runs only while separating; copy stems out and play from diskPlausible but depends on the engine's disk layout, and it overlaps the recovery work in idea 6. Folded into idea 6's lighter steps.
5Saved-stems storage view, FLAC transcode, LRU evictionToo expensive relative to value for a first release; pruning of missing jobs already exists. FLAC decode in the webview is unverified.
6Block-streamed mix renderer to lift the 360 s cap, plus multi-track exportLarge change that rests on windowed WAV decode in the webview and the unverified WebCodecs assumption. Idea 5 delivers most of the benefit with less risk.
7Record your own take with echo cancellation and a ghost takeScope overrun: a new product area. No code uses getUserMedia, and microphone permission in the Tauri webview plus cancellation quality are unproven.
8"Time to first practice" bundle (practice-first separation, owned model download, lazy engine)Bundles two weak ideas and one that strains the unmodified-engine principle. The uncontroversial part, keeping synth practice usable during setup, is kept in idea 6.
9Share a practice sheet as a file; spaced resurfacing of shaky barsKept only as later extensions of idea 3. Both stretch toward the deferred song library.
10Replace six media elements with one audio context outrightThe plan's own larger fallback; kept only as a gated contingency inside idea 7.
