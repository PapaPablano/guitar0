Read this first. Every idea that keeps the YouTube picture in the file depends on one unrecorded fact: whether screen capture (getDisplayMedia, and the element-crop APIs) works inside the Tauri WebView2 shell. The spike meant to answer it, dev/capture-spike.ts, has no recorded result. Idea 2 answers it first and unblocks the rest.

Codebase Context

Today's "Record with video" (src/export/record-capture.ts, src/app/useRecordWithVideo.ts) screen-captures the live window with getDisplayMedia and MediaRecorder. It requests 30 fps, fixes video at 8 Mbps and audio at 192 kbps, and sets no resolution, so the file is as large as whatever window or tab the player picked. The browser picker appears every time and the player must tick "share tab audio". The recording runs in real time from 0, forces the loop off and tempo to 1x, ignores the saved video offset, and can include the record banner and dialog in the frame. Leaving the page discards it. The blob is held in memory until stop.

The offline export is a different path. renderStageFrame (src/render/stage-frame.ts:98) is pure in time, encoder.worker.ts encodes it with WebCodecs at 1920x1080 at 60 fps or 1080x1920 at 30 fps, faster than real time, and it honors the video offset through tabTimeAt. It never draws the YouTube dock or carries YouTube sound (plan requirement R11 and AE5, amended on 2026-10-25 only to let the recorded export show the dock).

The YouTube iframe is cross-origin, so its picture can reach a file only through display capture. Canvas reads and WebCodecs cannot get at it. The owner has accepted the YouTube terms and copyright risk and chose to proceed without a written source on 2026-10-25. The legal review of "Record with video" and the payment-provider policy check are still open in docs/launch-checklist.md. The YouTube developer policies do not name screen recording. They bar storing copies of the content (III.E.1) and separating or modifying its audio or video (III.I.7), and a legal reading of that is not made.

Outside signal (moderate): Mediabunny is the successor to mp4-muxer and drives WebCodecs from a canvas deterministically. Region Capture and Element Capture crop a display-capture track to one element but work only on self-capture and are unverified in WebView2. Windows Graphics Capture is the standard native way to capture a WebView2 window, BitBlt returns a white frame, and no ready-made Tauri plugin exists. Soundslice shares by link and keeps user recordings audio-only. Nothing found on how Synthesia, Yousician or Rocksmith export video.

Idea 1 · Axis 2 Frame fidelity

1. Keyed slot: offline-quality stage, live dock only

Stop recording the whole window. Render the highway or neck through the existing deterministic renderStageFrame path at true 1080p60 (or vertical), with a reserved 200x200 slot where the dock sits. In one real-time pass, capture only the dock (its picture and sound) and log a clock trace of tab time against wall time, including buffering holds. Composite the dock clip into the slot and mux its audio. Because the stage is re-rendered from the trace, a stall in the video leaves the stage in step with it.

Basis
direct: stage-frame.ts:95-96 "The video export draws every frame through this function, so a frame matches what the page shows at that time." direct: the record path requests frameRate: 30 (record-capture.ts:67), fixes 8 Mbps (:85), and takes no alignment (useRecordWithVideo.ts:13-18). direct: plan E14 "Quality depends on window size and screen capture, below the offline render." direct: youtube-clock.ts:297-298 the views wait while the video loads. external: Region and Element Capture crop a display-capture track to one element, self-capture only. reasoned: live TV records each source clean beside the program mix, so the clean layer never inherits the weakest source's quality.
Rationale
It is the only candidate that makes the file visibly better than a screen record: crisp 60 fps stage, no browser chrome or banner, the offset honored, and a vertical variant from the same pass. YouTube pixels stay only in the small dock area, which is the exposure the owner already accepted.
Downsides
The hardest idea here. It needs same-tab capture plus Region Capture working in WebView2 (a window-surface pick cannot be cropped). Retiming the dock clip against the offline frames is tricky because the embed reports its position in coarse steps (youtube-clock.ts:49-50), and audio can glitch across stalls. The dock is only 200x200, so the video stays small. It still puts YouTube picture and sound in the file, so R11 and AE5 need the same amendment the recorded export got.

Idea 2 · Axis 1 Capture mechanics

2. Capture doctor, clean audio and guarded start

Make the real-time pass a reliable step before making it prettier, and make its sound clean. Promote dev/capture-spike into a one-time "capture doctor" that probes WebView2 (display capture, tab audio, element crop) and records which method works, behind one capture-source interface so the recorder, spike and later methods share the same codec, bitrate and chunk settings.

Clean audio. The recorder asks for audio: true with no audio options, so the browser may run echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain on captured music. Request the audio with those three processing stages off, and have the doctor record which of them are actually active in WebView2, the audio sample rate, and whether the track carries the tab's sound only or the whole system's. The doctor makes a short test clip with processing on and off so the player or developer can compare. Raise the audio bitrate only if the test shows the codec, not the processing, is the limit.

At record time, check the stream right after the picker, before the clock moves: an audio track is present, the shared surface is this window, the dock is in the frame, the player is ready. Then run a short countdown. Add a leave-page guard, and lock the dock in place and block covering it while recording, as plan U4 asks.

Basis
direct: useRecordWithVideo.ts:81-82 clock.seek(0); clock.play(); runs with no wait for player readiness. direct: the spike labels the missing audio case 'NONE (tick "Share tab audio" in the picker)' and nothing checks it. direct: the spike and the shipped recorder differ in fallback list, bitrate (6 versus 8 Mbps) and chunk length, and the MIME list is duplicated. direct: plan E18 "The dock must stay visible, unobstructed and unminimized for the whole recording (R8)." direct: no beforeunload guard exists in src/. direct: record-capture.ts:67-69 every capture request is audio: true with no audio constraints, and :85 records at audioBitsPerSecond: 192_000. reasoned: Chromium can apply echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain to captured audio unless they are switched off, which would flatten or pump music; YouTube's audio is also already lossy, so the record adds a second lossy pass. Which of these is active in WebView2 was not verified, which is why the doctor measures it.
Rationale
A real-time export means one mistake costs the whole song length, and today the failures are silent: a forgotten checkbox, a wrong surface, a covered dock. This also turns the biggest unknown into a recorded fact, which decides whether ideas 1, 5 and 6 are worth building. The player named audio as where the quality is lost, and none of the other ideas change the audio path. Turning off the processing is a one-line change to test first.
Downsides
Apart from the audio, it does not change how the file looks. The audio cause is a hypothesis until the test clip is compared. If processing is not the cause, the remaining loss is the double lossy encode, which only idea 4 (no YouTube sound in the file) avoids. The verifier judged the streamed-to-disk piece of the original candidate cosmetic for song-length recordings, so only the leave-page guard is kept. The doctor needs a person at the picker once per environment.

Idea 3 · Axis 3 Time and flow

3. Clip recording: a loop or section at a tempo step

Drop the rule that every recording starts at 0 and lasts the whole song. Let the recording cover the current loop or a chosen section, at one of the 25-point tempo steps the YouTube player can follow, with a short count-in and the clip length shown first. A 15-second riff costs 15 seconds. Whole-song export stays with the offline path.

Basis
direct: useRecordWithVideo.ts:72 "A loop would wrap the song before the end, and a tempo change would make the recording a different length," followed by setLoop(null) and setRate(1). direct: tempo-steps.ts:1 lists speeds the embedded player offers, so the video can always follow. direct: dev/etsy-ads/videos/etsy-15s/ holds 15-second cuts, and launch-checklist.md:55 lists a demo video as open. direct: README.md:13 "loops and tempo changes apply to practice only."
Rationale
The real-time cost only hurts because the unit is the whole song. A clip turns the weakness into a feature and makes the app's core, loops and slowed tempo, exportable. It needs no new capture technology.
Downsides
The loop wrap and seek settle have to be handled so the take starts clean (SETTLE_SECONDS exists for seeks). Short clips make the picker friction a larger share of the effort, so it pairs with idea 2.

Idea 4 · Axis 4 YouTube rules

4. Studio package: stage with a reserved dock slot and a sync sidecar

A second export tier that contains no YouTube at all. Run the offline render with an empty 200x200 slot where the dock sits, and write a small sidecar with the video ID, saved offset, alignment, tempo and link. The player layers the real video in an editor, or plays it beside the file. It runs at offline speed and keeps R11 and AE5 untouched. It sits beside the recorded export as the safe default, the way a commentary track plays against a film the buyer already owns.

Basis
direct: AE5 "an export with a video attached... shows no YouTube content." direct: launch-checklist.md:44 the legal review of "Record with video" is open, and the checklist also lists the payment-provider YouTube policy. external: YouTube developer policies III.E.1 and III.I.7 (no storing copies, no separating audio or video); not placing the content in the file fits the text most cleanly. direct: audio.ts:68-70 already encodes the offset mapping.
Rationale
It is the hedge if the legal read or the payment provider goes badly, and the only fast path that is clean under the policy text. It reuses the saved offset the recorder currently ignores.
Downsides
On its own it is a step back from what was asked: the user still has to put the video in. The verifier rated it sound as a hedge but a downgrade as the main answer. A hosted watch page was considered and cut because the app has no sharing infrastructure.

Idea 5 · Axis 2 Frame fidelity

5. Clean frame: crop the shot to stage and dock

Keep the whole-window recorder but fix what is in the shot. Hide RecordBanner and the export dialog from the captured pixels and show the counter somewhere outside the frame, such as the window title. Where Region or Element Capture works, crop the track to the stage-plus-dock container so browser chrome falls outside, and fix the output to a preset aspect ratio.

Basis
direct: RecordBanner.tsx:22 is a fixed DOM span "Recording {clockText…}" and the capture records whatever the page shows. direct: the record path has no preset, and its size is whatever window was picked. external: Chrome documents Region Capture and Element Capture for self-capture, including a cross-origin-iframe pattern.
Rationale
It is the cheap improvement that does not need the full rebuild in idea 1, and the banner-hiding part works even if the crop APIs do not exist in WebView2.
Downsides
The crop needs same-tab capture, which preferCurrentTab only hints at, and the picker remains. It does not fix 30 fps, the fixed bitrate or real-time cost, and it overlaps idea 1 if that is built.

Idea 6 · Axis 1 Capture mechanics

6. Native Windows capture of the app window

If WebView2 cannot capture itself, add a Rust command in src-tauri that captures the app window through Windows Graphics Capture, with the window's own audio, and hands the MP4 to the existing 4 MiB-slice save path. No picker, no tick box, steady 60 fps, and it works when the window is partly covered. It would be the first capture module in the shell and could later serve practice-session clips and marketing videos.

Basis
direct: plan E17 "If the desktop shell cannot capture itself, stop and report. The fallback is a native capture path in src-tauri, a much larger job." direct: src-tauri has no capture code. external: Windows Graphics Capture captures GPU-composited WebView2 where BitBlt gives a white frame; the Rust windows-capture crate captures via WGC and encodes MP4 (GStreamer merge request 4631, OBS issue 13359). Per-process audio loopback is from general knowledge and was not verified.
Rationale
It removes the picker and the audio checkbox by construction, and it is the only route if the spike shows the browser path fails on desktop.
Downsides
New Rust, new audio capture and a yellow capture border (removable on newer Windows only). No Tauri plugin exists. It fixes the picker, not real-time cost. A covered dock would break the unobstructed-player rule. Windows only. Build only if idea 2's doctor says the browser route fails.

Idea 7 · Axis 5 Destination and purpose

7. Finishing pass: a clean, fast-start, attributed file

After a recording stops, run it through a lightweight pass (WebCodecs or a Mediabunny remux, not a bundled ffmpeg) that normalizes the container to MP4/H.264 with fast start, a constant frame rate and the chosen preset size, trims the leading silence, and applies the saved offset. The same pass adds a small credit for the video (title, channel, link) that never covers the dock, plus a sidecar with the source URL and offset, and applies the Made with Tab Highway mark when the tier needs it.

Basis
direct: the container is chosen by the browser, with a webm fallback name (record-capture.ts:24,32-34), and MediaRecorder output is fragmented. direct: no watermark logic exists on the record path. external: YouTube developer policy III.F.2(c) bars obscuring attribution. external: Mediabunny is the successor to mp4-muxer with deterministic canvas output.
Rationale
It brings the recorded file up to the destination quality of the offline export and shows good faith toward the platform while the legal read is pending.
Downsides
The verifier called it mixed: remux and trim are real needs, but the watermark is dead code while isPro is forced true, and the credit is tiny work. A bundled ffmpeg brings size and licensing cost. A re-encode of recorded YouTube content is the riskiest reading of the policy text.
